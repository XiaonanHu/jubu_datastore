# Segment Writing Prompt — v1 (ARCHIVED, produced the pilot stories)

**Status: superseded by `segment_writing_v2.md`.** Kept verbatim so prompt
changes can be reviewed and so the 22 pilot stories can be traced to the
rules that produced them. Do not write new stories with this.

This is the writing instruction actually used to author the pilot stories
(distilled from the authoring briefs given to the writing sessions).

---

## Story assignment
You are writing a branching children's story for age 5-6, heard aloud via
text-to-speech, not read. You are given: the topic, its per-age framing,
vocabulary and avoid list, the story pattern, the teller, and the tone
recipe (dominant + optional accent carried by one character).

## Structure rules
- Exactly 6 segments (s1, s2a, s2b, s3, s4a, s4b) and 2 choice points:
  s1 → c1 (rejoins) → s2a or s2b → s3 (shared) → c2 (splits) → s4a or s4b.
- **Segment length: 150-200 words each (~700 words per path).**
- 1-2 slots with `{slot}` placeholders; shared segments must read correctly
  from either branch.
- Say-words: 1-2 concrete words per option, the two must not sound alike.
- Options must differ in kind, not size.

## Writing rules
- SPEAKABLE: short complete sentences (mostly under 15 words), no ellipses,
  no asterisks, no parentheses, no emojis, no stage directions.
- "We" voice throughout (Buju + child adventuring together), or the
  teller's frame. Never address the child by name.
- Every segment keeps something in motion: what we are reaching for right
  now (concrete, never a feeling-word) and what is gently unresolved.
- Show feelings through body and behaviour; at this age a feeling may be
  plainly named if paired with shown behaviour.
- **Vivid and concrete always**: "a hungry place in the sky that gobbles up
  even light", never abstract lecture.
- Cartoon-safe, never scary. Both endings warm, fully resolved, no
  cliffhangers, carrying one small discovery home.
- Use the topic's vocabulary naturally; never include anything on the avoid
  list.
- Never use: assessment, mastery, score, milestone, behind, ahead of,
  delayed, gifted, grade level.

## Known failures of this version (from expert review, July 2026)

1. **No opening rule** — stories begin mid-action with no priming: who "we"
   are, where we are, why we are going in.
2. **Word quota caused padding** — "150-200 words" plus "always vivid"
   produced decorative description with no job to do.
3. **No test for whether a description earns its place** — scenery,
   arrivals ("out from under a rock ledge pops..."), and colour details
   that touch nothing in the story.
4. **Uniform sentence length** — "mostly under 15 words" flattened rhythm;
   no short punch sentences, no long flowing ones.
5. **Narrator-led, not dialogue-led** — the companion decorates instead of
   participating; the narrator asks the choice question instead of a
   character.
6. **Similes drawn from anywhere** — including adult or abstract imagery,
   rather than from a child's daily world.
7. **Comic business repeated** — hat falls off, hat slides, spark on nose,
   crossed eyes: gags stacked in place of story movement.
8. **Adult mood words allowed** — "cozy warm", "magical", which tell a
   feeling instead of giving a child something to feel.
9. **Unused setups** — objects and views introduced and never used again.
10. **Narrated travel** — stairs spiralling up and down instead of arriving.
